# NEON SOVEREIGN — The Story (v3, built on Jesse's outline)

*Spoilers included. Tone: PG-13. Romantic, funny, heroic and, at the end, sad. Rex is kept exactly as he was. The music theme, the letters and the over-the-top cast carry over from v1. The protagonist and the plot are new and follow Jesse's outline.*

---

## The world: Halcyon

Halcyon is the 80s as Final Fantasy would do it: neon cities with monorails, muscle cars and arcades, sitting alongside swords, sorcery, airships, monsters in the wilds and ancient temples. It's always a little bit sunset.

**Music is magic.** Everything in Halcyon hums with **the Resonance**, the song underneath the world. Singers, guitarists and organists can shape it, and the best of them are basically wizards. Every bar band in Halcyon is a little bit dangerous.

**The Canticle.** For three thousand years, every great event in Halcyon has been foretold in the Canticle, a prophecy written in **red ink** by an order of sisters called **the Rubric**. Every prophecy in the Canticle has come true, and kings are crowned by it. Its final verse says that a great Darkness will rise, that a **Hero of Legend** will be named to strike it down, and that a **Bringer of End** will come. The Canticle also contains a clause nobody pays attention to: *only the Hero's hand may still the Dark.*

Halcyon has a lot of neon, and nobody has ever been free. Nobody has noticed that either.

## The cast

### EDDAR SOIPON, the protagonist (19)
- **Look:** lean, sharp-featured, dark hair always falling in his eyes, a beat-up denim jacket covered in hand-drawn patches, fingerless gloves, a pencil behind his ear and a battered notebook in his back pocket. His eyes turn faintly violet when he's angry, and he doesn't know they do.
- **Who he is:** a true nobody. He grew up in St. Cadence Orphanage and has no memory of anything before age six. He mops floors at the Pink Wyvern, a rock club, and cleans the Hall of Records at night, where he has secretly read the entire Canticle. He's the smartest person in Solace Bay, and nobody has noticed. He's kind, curious and funny in a dry, under-the-breath way. He thinks life is fundamentally unfair, and he keeps a running tally to prove it.
- **His device: the Unfairness Ledger.** Eddar's notebook pops up on screen as handwritten margin notes. *"Entry #2,207: Rex got named Hero of Legend while drunk on a stage. I got named 'that mop kid' while sober on a floor."* It's funny, it's sharp, and over the game it slowly turns into a love story. (The last entry is in the epilogue.)
- **His power:** small strange things happen around him. Pencils drift, lights flicker, and a bully's punch stops an inch from his face. He thinks he's lucky. **He is the product of three thousand years of the Rubric's secret bloodline engineering**, the being the sisters bred to sing the Canticle's last verse. At six, in a fit of rage, he destroyed his temple and his birth town. The Rubric purged his memory and hid him in an orphanage. **Nobody in the prophecies mentions him.** He's the wildcard.
- **What the bullies call him:** "Adder." It's a twist of his name and a joke about how harmless he is, and it becomes very unfunny by Act V.

### REX VALOR, the bully and Hero of Legend (22)
*Kept exactly as written.* Huge, gorgeous, a golden quiff and a white leather jacket with gold pauldrons. He fights with his fists, "Ol' One and Two" (Jesse, 2026-11-05: no lance; it's called jousting because a bout opens with a straight-armed fist bump, like crossed lances), he's a three-time Grand Joust champion, and he refers to himself in the third person ("Rex doesn't *do* 'no'."). He shoves Eddar into trash barrels, and lays out anyone *else* who tries: "Nobody pushes Adder around but me." **He can't read.** He pays Eddar to write his love letters to Princess Lyra.
- **New:** the Rubric names Rex **the Hero of Legend**, and he is spectacularly bad at it. He's vain, he chases women, he parties, he shows up hungover to destiny, and he'd rather win the princess than save the world. Underneath all that is a guy who's terrified of not being enough, and who only ever had one person he could be honest with: the kid he bullies.

### SABLE, the ronin monk (25)
She arrives in town on a black light-cycle, in a glossy black bodysuit under a matte black trench coat, with long white-silver hair. She's a sorceress of sorts and carries no weapon, ever. She has no filter: "You're staring. Either say something or stop."
- **Secret:** she's a **Rubric sister**. She survived the day Eddar, at six, destroyed the temple she was a novice in. For thirteen years she has been his secret watcher, ordered to report on him and to "silence" him if he ever woke up. She has never told him any of this. She has also never filed a report that wasn't a lie to protect him.
- She officially comes to Solace Bay to escort the Hero of Legend. Unofficially, she's there because Eddar's power is waking up.
- **Her word is "inevitable"** (Jesse, 2026-11-06). At some point Eddar says it back to her, in a cute display of affection.

### ROXANNE "ROXY" VEGA (21)
- **Look:** red-and-black leather jacket, ripped tights, flame-pattern high-top sneakers (never roller skates), and a big 80s ponytail that **literally ignites** when she's angry. She plays a flame-red guitar that's also a weapon.
- **Fixed motivation:** she's the frontwoman of **Roxy & the Wildfires**, a bar band with a real shot at greatness. Since her dad left, she has been sending half of every paycheck home to her mom and three little brothers. She has no patience because she has never had time for any. She's *not* entitled. She's exhausted, ambitious and fiercely loyal, and she's the one who stands up for Eddar when Rex isn't around.
- **Why "anyone but him":** Roxy falls for drama: the dangerous, the loud, the guys who might wreck her. First Rex, then a pirate airship captain, then Thunderhorn's swaggering former bassist. Eddar is "the one guy who's always there," which to Roxy means family, not romance. She only understands what that meant too late, and she writes the song for his funeral.

### SIR VANCE THUNDERHORN (54)
A washed-up glam-metal paladin and former legend, with a towering silver mane, battered chrome armor and a flying-V guitar-axe. He can't hit the high note anymore. He's over the top, warm and hilarious. He's the first adult to look at Eddar and say, "Kid, you're the headliner."
- **Body (Jesse, 2026-11-06, for the Unreal build).** No longer overweight. Playtest feedback asked why the men could have an overweight hero while every woman was a runway model. Fitting the 80s, Vance now has an action-hero, Rambo-style physique. Everything else stays the same, except his chrome breastplate opens at the front like a vest to show the muscle beneath.
- **His sunset (Jesse, 2026-11-06).** His rock-star days are years behind him, but he doesn't want to let them go. At all. His band, **Velvet Kane** (Jesse, 2026-11-06), dumped him years ago when he got too far into Neon Drinks, and he hasn't won a battle of the bands in twenty years. He drinks **Neon Red Scotch**, never Neon Pink. He joins Psychedelic Titanium for the Act I battle of the bands, then the party (see Act I).
- **His quest: one last ballad.** Thunderhorn has spent fifteen years trying to write **the one** — a mega, soul-lifting power ballad that will make a stadium of strangers hold hands. He scribbles lyrics on napkins, armor plates, and other people's maps. He gathers it from the journey: a line from Roxy's fight, a chord from the Reef, a verse from watching Eddar and Sable on the deck. **The ballad is missing one word** at the peak of the chorus. He can't find it all game. (It's "love." He finds it at Eddar's death — see the Epilogue.) When he premieres the ballad at the Last Dance, he still doesn't know it, but each time he goes to sing the word, something visual fills the screen, such as two people kissing or other acts of love, so the viewer knows what it is.

### PRINCESS LYRA SOLARIS (23)
Heir to the sky-capital Solara. She's sharp, poised and political. Out in the world she wears a red, Tron-inspired bodysuit, because she likes the attention, and fights with a throwing disc (also Tron-inspired). Her gown of silver light and starlight veil are for special occasions only. She **falls in love with the letters "Rex" sends her**, which Eddar writes, and **she chooses the Hero of Legend.** She works out that Rex didn't write them, but not that Eddar did. She admires Rex's willingness to stand against impossible odds, even at the cost of his life, and finds it attractive. But whenever he opens his mouth ("doll" and the like), she turns to ice. She finds out too late whose words she fell for.
- **Habit:** she blows her bangs out of her eyes. At some point the ever-direct Sable asks her about it:
  - Sable: "Why don't you use a headband?"
  - Lyra: "Guys like it."
  - Sable, matter-of-fact: "You could get any man in the world. Why do you care what they like?"
  - Lyra: "This is less risky than talking to a dude... and finding out they're like Rex the buffoon, or Eddar the brooding menace, or Grubb the buffoon because he doesn't realise Rex is a buffoon... you get the point."
  - Sable, smirking at Lyra, who is smirking back: "Telling them to blow off works for me."

### GRUBB, Rex's sidekick (Jesse, 2026-11-05)
- **Yellow chakra, the chakra of action.** Grubb is an action taker. He runs a trading business and has contacts, mundane ones (he knows who to talk to for good prices on salt). He knows most people in town and has made acquaintances here and there.
- **A human doing, not a human being.** He was always thinking about acquiring more, building his business, optimising this or that, and he felt increasingly empty.
- **The Celestine Married (revised by Jesse, 2026-11-05).** Years ago Grubb's sister **Priya**, her husband **Abdul** and her two children, **Arya** and **Rohan**, sailed on the ocean-going vessel *Celestine Married* to see Grubb's mother **Simran**, who was dying. Grubb should have been with them, but what he believes was a strange sickness left him weak and then comatose, so he couldn't sail. It wasn't a sickness (Jesse, 2026-11-05): he was filled with discordant energy. He'll remark on his fevered dreams: broken bells crashing, out-of-tune tubas blasting, a violin in desperate need of tuning being strung violently. He saw blood, endless blood; heroes and villains coming and going, living and dying like notes in a long symphony; everyone dancing happily on strings of light; silence consuming millions of families, whole species going extinct, and other species arising as a new act began, where new families could dance. (Vivid, cryptic imagery mixed with music.) They were never heard from again. Months later fishermen came across the ship drifting out of a fog: fully intact, everything on board intact, but no crew, no passengers, and no sign of struggle or attack. It became a bizarre ghost story. Grubb has silently cursed the gods for saving the wrong sibling. The story later reveals he was saved because of his role in the Canticle. His already empty life lost even its reason for being empty.
- **His name.** "Grubb" isn't his real name. It's what he kept after Rex kept calling him "grub" when they first met.
- **Aubade.** He's relatively young and never married, and he has a big, shy crush on Aubade. It's cute and funny: he's no ladies' man and he looks up to Rex, while she's cold and serene, and her former husband is Nocturne, the Scion of Silence. It helps him get over what happened.
- **Rex, his life preserver.** Less than half a year later he met Rex at a jousting competition. Rex, his polar opposite, never thinks about anything, yet always seems to come out on top: he always seems to know what's best, life is great, and he's such an idiot. With Rex there's never a dull moment, so Grubb never has to distract himself. There's also the fast life: free booze, adoring fans (Rex's, but Rex keeps Grubb around because he's such a capable butt-kisser, so Grubb is no stranger to groupies). He isn't a swimfan in the usual sense; his mind, on the brink of implosion, found a life preserver.
- **How others see him.** Roxy thinks Grubb is just a swimfan (Rex has a few from the jousting competitions). Eddar senses there's more to the story, and loves Grubb all the same. Away from Rex, people look down on both of them. Eddar maybe gets it worse, but Grubb at least understands what it's like to be a loser.
- **DLC:** finding Grubb's family could be part of it.
- **Slam poetry (Jesse, 2026-11-06).** Grubb is into slam poetry. It's usually ridiculous, but out of it comes his story about losing his sister, nephew and niece.
  - **His money stays a secret.** Grubb is a well-to-do man. None of the heroes ever find out about his finances; the player only learns it by putting things together. It doesn't affect the story.
  - **"Rub a Dub Grubb."** He enters the battle of the bands (Act I) by himself, without telling anyone. See Act I.
  - **"You took the wrong one."** His heartfelt performance in Act II or III, and the start of Aubade opening up to him. See Act II.
  - **Aubade.** Until that performance his yearning for her is obvious, innocent and juvenile. Before it, she usually ignores him or says something in rhyme about him kissing Rex's butt some more. The romance pays off at the Last Dance (Act V), and the optional ring scene with Nocturne follows it.
  - **DLC:** if there is DLC, Grubb proposes marriage (in the way Aubade's people do it) and gives her the ring back.

### WAKA DAKA, the drummer (Jesse, 2026-11-06)
The first of the five new heroes. Real name **Jimmy Blowfeld**: an orphan who didn't like the name the orphanage gave him, and changed it to Waka Daka when he became a drummer. He tells everyone he'll smash 'em if they call him anything but his drummer name. His ex-wife Martha calls him Jim.
- **Look.** Male, dark Latino, 6'3", really lanky. He looks crazed, with an anime-inspired spiky hairstyle streaked in various neon colours. Covered in white tattoos, lots of piercings. He drums bare-chested, in khakis ten sizes too large that would fall off him if not for a rope belt. Across his chest: **"Martha my Angle"** (not Angel; the tattoo artist couldn't spell).
- **Drumming.** Wild and frantic, like Animal, the Muppets' drummer.
- **His old band's poster** hangs in the Pink Wyvern, with Waka Daka front and centre (Jesse, 2026-11-06). The band is **The Paradiddles** (approved by Jesse, 2026-11-06; a paradiddle is a basic drum rudiment). Still touring, and pretty big back when Waka Daka was in it. Its humble name stands out among the crazy ones (Velvet Kane, Psychedelic Titanium), the way "The Beatles" does.
- **Backstory.** Martha was his band's lead vocalist and his life partner. He was a great drummer once, but he went down the Neon Booze route, which cost his band a lot of boos and flung rotten tomatoes. She gave him an ultimatum, and he couldn't keep away from the Neon Drink. She's now with the band's new drummer.
- **How the player learns it.** From dialogue throughout the world, some of it story-related (including running into his old band, and his ex-wife). It can also be read in **his Journals**, which unlock hero points for him to spend on his Abilities.
- **In the party.** He speaks mostly in nonsense. The party members largely feel sorry for him. Roxy recognises him from his glory days. He doesn't really compete for space and attention with the other heroes, and he's really quite funny.
- **In battle.** He fights with his drumsticks and is really fast. He has no unique relationship ability with anyone, but he starts with a passive that prevents any debuff that would make him lose control (Sleep, Berserk and so on): he's already lost his mind, and can't lose it again.
- **Arc: recovery and redemption.** By Act V he speaks like an English gentleman ("Bond, James Bond" sort of deal). He's the drummer at the Last Dance, where he's introduced as Jimmy Blowfeld. Martha is there as a guest, and at some point in the night she hugs him and gushes about how much he's changed. Some of this is resolved before the Last Dance, building up to that moment.
- **Joins:** as the eighth character in Act I, in the next town after Solace Bay (see Act I). On the first playthrough he isn't in Roxy's band for the battle of the bands, because he's lost to Neon Drink at that point. In Encore and chapter select, the English-gentleman version of him can play in it.
- **Not Vance again.** Vance's arc is fading into the sunset and not wanting to let go; his song plays over the credits. Waka Daka's is having lost everything to Neon Drink. Vance can still drink it like most people. Waka gives it up.

### THE RADIO PIRATE (Jesse, 2026-11-06; name to be chosen)
The second of the five new heroes. Name ideas, the ocean and continent names, her backstory and contraptions are in Claude's draft "Radio Pirate and Birtha: ideas" (Claude Doc), awaiting Jesse.
- **Look.** Distinctly Persian/Arab, with entrancing green eyes. She wears silks and sashes, lots of purple silk, and a long silk ribbon holds her hair back in a waist-long braided ponytail. No neon or the usual flare: small diodes of gold light in her hair, and strange sigils on her silken back, also alive with golden glow. Wild eyes. Not very big, about 5'5", skinny like Roxy, though the silks hide it. She contrasts visually with everyone else, which builds that she's from a far-off land.
- **Where she's from.** Across the ocean (name to be chosen), from the other continent, which the Hush hit before this one because it's closer to Nocturne's archipelago. She could join in early Act II: a survivor familiar with the Hush, with a real, civilian perspective, unlike Sable's and Leagues' cosmic ones.
- **Personality.** Intelligent, full of energy, with a cute little laugh, but a very quiet person. Often humming a tune; doesn't say much.
- **Her ney.** She plays the ney (the ancient Middle Eastern flute). When she plays it at camp, it's the music that plays during Camp, and it's ethereal in its beauty. **The ney always plays at camp** (Jesse, 2026-11-06): it's the only ethereal instrument in the game, and it sets camp apart from the crazy, neon-lit world and its big sound and big everything. It never goes silent.
- **In combat (direction).** A summoner, of a kind: she summons contraptions she's built into battle. Not giant summons, but things that are part musical contraption, part 80s robotics: aggro magnets and the like.
- **Her arc.** Like Waka Daka's, not intimately tied to everyone else's literally, but with deep emotional attachment. As Grubb is to Rex, she is to Eddar as he climbs in power: Eddar is someone who can avenge her people and crush the evil that fell upon her land. Her hatred and wrath consume her. As she watches what starts to happen with Eddar, she begins to let go of those she lost. As Grubb finally awakens to Rex, she does to Eddar. Perhaps late in Act V she tells him: "I'm not asking for vengeance anymore... It won't bring my baby brother back, it won't put songs and laughter back into my chrome cobblestone streets... I've... I... I have forgiven Nocturne... It's what my ancestors would want me to do... and it's what I must do so this rage doesn't consume me... ... ... as it is you." Camera cuts to Eddar, his eyes bloodshot from holding back tears, as this gentle reprimand from his swimfan cuts deep. Real deep.

### BIRTHA LEADBELCHER (Jesse, 2026-11-06)
The third of the five new heroes. "I eat lead for breakfast." Build-out ideas are in Claude's draft "Radio Pirate and Birtha: ideas", awaiting Jesse.
- **Look.** A big, strong lady. Dark black skin, cornrows, too much makeup, lots of jewellery, all of it interwoven with neon (she likes big colours).
- **A bouncer,** with no musical instrument.
- **Her fear.** She doesn't like the dark, and she's terrified of any fog or mist. She's the only survivor of a ship taken out at sea by something unexplainable. She can't remember; it's like she doesn't want to. At first the player assumes it was Nocturne, his kaiju or the like. Then Grubb performs "You took the wrong one" and, afterwards, explains it, and Birtha freezes: the *Celestine Married* was the ship she was on.
- **Rex.** She thinks Rex is a hunk. Rex is secretly afraid of her: "She eats lead for breakfast.. I wouldn't even be an appetiser..."

### MAESTRO NOCTURNE, the Great Evil
- **Look:** tall, with a mirror-chrome featureless mask and a long black coat crawling with silent static. He conducts with a baton of black light. His soldiers, **the Hush**, are masked knights who carry the **Dirge** with them: where they march, every song in the area collapses into one low, endless funeral drone, colours drain, and people stand frozen like statues, mouths open as if mid-song. (Players *hear* an area being taken: the town theme literally decays into the Dirge.)
- **He always speaks in verse (Jesse, 2026-11-04).** His sentences come in pairs, like song lyrics: each pair ends on rhyming words and has roughly the same number of syllables. (His draft lines elsewhere in this document are content, not final wording; they get set in verse when scripted.)
- **What he wants:** to **end the Canticle**. He believes it's a cage that has scripted every life in Halcyon for three thousand years. His method is to silence the world by conquest. He's ruthless, and he has a code: he keeps his word, he spares children, and he has never once harmed someone who surrendered.
- **What he knows:** the rules of the Canticle better than anyone. He does **not** know who Eddar is until late in the story.
- **He brings the Hush to end the prophecy, not to fulfil it.** "The Scion of Silence" is only what the legend calls it; he and most others say "the Hush". (Optional late dialogue only: he loves funeral dirges, and is mildly put out that people call the Dirge the end of music, when a dirge is, technically, music.)

### THE RUBRIC
A Bene Gesserit-style order of sisters in red, with ink-stained hands and scarlet hoods, led by **Mother Superior Carmine**. They write the Canticle, and quietly they make it come true. Eddar is their masterpiece and their greatest fear.

## The love web
```
EDDAR ──▶ ROXY ──▶ REX ──▶ LYRA ──▶ "the letters" (secretly EDDAR)
  ▲                                       
  └──── SABLE (has loved him for years; they finally kiss at the Last Dance, Act V)
```
**It must not be obvious who Eddar ends up with.** Sable is the slow burn: glances, hands that brush, interruptions, and one near-kiss after another. Along the way the player can pursue real romantic tension with **Roxy** and with **Princess Lyra**, up to a kiss with either one (see "Romance paths" below). That's deliberate. It makes the game morally grey, because Lyra is Rex's princess and Roxy was Rex's girl, and it keeps the Sable build-up tense right up until the dance.
Eddar starts out wanting Roxy, who loves Rex. Rex wants Lyra. Lyra loves the man in the letters, and that man is Eddar. Sable, the one person who has watched over him all along, is the one he finally sees.

## Romance paths (player choice, built up across Acts I–III)
Romance choices are made in camp scenes, side quests and key dialogue. **Bonds** track them (DESIGN.md §5.2). The main beats stay fixed: Sable is where the story lands at the Last Dance, and Jesse's ending is canon. Before that, nothing is settled, and the player can go as far as a kiss.
- **Roxy: "Always There."** The tension is about timing. She's into anyone but him, until she isn't. There's a late-night rooftop after her disastrous fling with Jack Cutlass (Act II) and a moment after the Siege of Solara when she finally *sees* him (Act III). If the player leans in, the **kiss** happens backstage in Act III, on the night her band plays for the refugees. It's wonderful, and it's too late, and both of them know it. It feeds her song in the epilogue.
- **Lyra: "The Letters."** This is the morally grey path. She fell in love with the letters Eddar wrote, and she's promised to the Hero of Legend. From Act III on, she suspects the truth. There's a balcony scene in Solara where she quotes a line and he finishes it. The player can choose **a kiss in the ruined palace library** (Act III), which is a betrayal of Rex, of her crown and of Sable's quiet hope. Nobody else sees it. Rex never finds out, unless the player tells him in Act V. That changes Rex's goodbye and the epilogue.
- **Sable: the slow burn.** Glances, sparring that runs a beat too long, a hand on the rail, interruptions, and her refusal to say what she is. It pays off at the Last Dance (Act V).
- **Consequences:** if you kissed Roxy or Lyra, Sable notices. Expect a short, devastatingly blunt exchange in Act III ("You kissed her. You're allowed. I'm not anyone's to be loyal to."), different Last Dance glances, and changed epilogue slides. None of these paths blocks the main story. They change what it costs.

## PROLOGUE — "Soundcheck" (≈1.5 h)

**Working script:** the storyboard doc "Storyboard: Prologue, The Pink Wyvern" (https://claude.ai/artifact/4psEFiu96kJco4nQCz1cun), v2 from Jesse's notes of 2026-11-03. It is authoritative for the opening up to the Bellshell fight.

**Solace Bay:** a neon coastal city of palm-lined boardwalks, arcades, monorails and rock clubs, built around the pipe-organ Cathedral of the Canticle. Tonight is **Canticle Night**, and the city is throwing **SAVE THE WORLD**, a battle of the bands held on the outskirts of town, the same town as the Pink Wyvern, in tents and stages like Coachella (Jesse, 2026-11-06). Everyone treats the name as a joke. SAVE THE WORLD is the official title; locals call it "the Save" or "Loudnight". Nobody (except Sable, the Rubric and the Soprano Order) knows the festival is the last folk memory of the real Naming of a Champion against the Scion of Silence.

**The Canticle is famous and unknown,** like the Rosetta Stone. People know there's an old prophecy and a "Champion", in many conflicting versions. They half-know the legend of the **Bringer of the Final End** the way people on Earth half-know Revelation.

**The Canticle of Life** has two verses. **Verse 1** (Leagues' voice-over in the prologue) says the Champion always defeats the Scion of Silence, and has since the world and its lifeblood, the music, began. It doesn't mention the Final End. **Verse 2** is told later, in voice-over, and speaks of the **Final End**. By then the player has met Nocturne and naturally assumes he is the Bringer of the Final End.

- **Opening at the Pink Wyvern:** the Wildfires rehearse for their heat, and the cold open plays out in speech bubbles: Roxy's pep talk, the band bickering, patrons heckling (a request for another band's song, "Take it off!", "Dedicate one to my daughter Tammy!"). Eddar, the ignored mop boy, mops, and says almost nothing; the floor is never done, because patrons keep spilling. Once the main puddles are clean, **Leagues Soprano** walks in through a locked door: a Miles Davis lookalike in a moth-eaten, gold-noted black robe and a black breastplate, with glowing white eyes. He sits in the corner and orders nothing. If spoken to: silence, then "Piss off," then "I told you to piss off." Lulu spills her cola by the stage ("You're a saint, Eddar." / "A saint who mops floors."), and only then does Roxy yell for him. Eddar fixes Roxy's amp (the punch and the violet flare). Roxy kisses his forehead.
- **The alley:** Rex's letter, the barrel, and "Nobody pushes Adder around but me." Vinnie returns with his dock crew, and **Rex and Eddar fight them together: the tutorial fight.** Three dockworkers trash-talk at first; once they turn Enhanced (never explained) the quips get angry: "No one makes me spit my own blood!", "Hey, these guys are kinda' tough. Even the weedy git.", "You guys are gunna' pay for that!" (DESIGN.md §6.5d).
- **The Champion:** back inside, Mama Dee orders Rex to throw out the freeloader. Leagues sees Rex and names him the **Champion of All Peoples, the Hero of Legend**. Rex takes it as natural (and as a better shot at Lyra). The regulars think Rex paid for it. The band calls it nonsense. Roxy: "Eddar makes a more believable champion than Rex, sheesh. No offence, Eddar." Leagues takes the spare guitar and plays, his music fills the band, and they play Roxy's impossible song "Burn Forever" (it needs two leads). In a one-minute cutscene titled "The Canticle of Life, Verse 1 of 2", Leagues' voice-over tells how the Champion always defeats the Scion of Silence. Rex never gets his title right ("Hero of All Peoples"). Mama Dee: "It's Hero of LEGEND, you fool!" Leagues: "Not magic. Prophecy." He agrees to join them at the heat, then leaves.
- **Monsters:** screaming outside. A woman enters, unnamed: tall, lithe, spray-on black under a long black trench, a mask over her whole face, long neon-silver hair (this is Sable, but the player doesn't learn her name yet). She looks at Eddar: "So you're the One? ...Let's see, Champion." Rex corrects her and Leagues confirms Rex. He bows slightly to Sable: the two belong to different orders that know of each other. To Rex, incredulous: "You're... the one?" Then: "This is... irrelevant. People won't save themselves. Get to it, champions." Before it arrives, a howl with static in it. Grubb, the only one of them who actually knows people outside the tavern, says something like: "That sounds like Old Bill's hound, but with something wrong with it..." (He's only comparing the sound to what he knows.) **Lamp Moths** are maddened moths that circle the neon in every explorable area (the Main Drag, Docks, Night Market, Arcade Pier and Cathedral Steps). They are harmless and can't be fought. **Monsters** are ordinary creatures of the world, driven mad by the Hush ("a world without music is like a body without breath"). Fights against them can end in mercy, with music calming them.

**Monster rules (Jesse, 2026-11-04):** most monsters are scary, and they're what the player fights regularly. Musical-themed monsters live only in specific parts of the world. An opening line, still to be drafted, is about becoming a monster by having monstrous thoughts.

### The Battle of the Bands (Jesse, 2026-11-06)
- **How it plays.** Likely 3 to 4 rounds, played like *Rock Band* or the dance and march sequences in FFVII Remake/Rebirth: buttons pressed to a beat. Each round is harder than the last. How Roxy's band does depends on the player's playing plus what they did to prepare, world events that can affect other contestants, and certain unlocks (for example, beating a given mini-boss unlocks a Special Charm that Lulu wears, raising her score when the player hits a drum beat on time). Spec in DESIGN.md §7.2.
- **The judges don't decide who wins.** The crowd does. The judges are there to stop cheating.
- **Before Round 1 (first playthrough).** Roxy goes to grab her guitar, and the camera pans over other guitars and instruments, with posters on the billboard above showing the likenesses of "Vance", "Aubade" and others. Roxy breaks the fourth wall: "If only this were Encore, we would all rock the night until it broke upon the dawn..."
- **Round 1: Roxy's band always wins**, even if the player deliberately blows every note. The opponents (some outlandish, over-the-top, bombastic and lengthy heavy-metal name, to be written) thought it was smart to put oversized, supercharged speakers right behind the judges' table. The speakers blow the judges off the table and humorously shock them, and the band is disqualified.
- **Round 2: vs "The Booming Biceps."** Easy: the player wins by hitting at least 50% of notes, with the story prep done, so most players get to experience Round 3's dialogue and story (nothing is gained from missing it). The Booming Biceps are goth muscleheads in too-tight tank tops and 80s-style short shorts. They're a pure vocals-and-drums group: no actual words, just vocals like a Gregorian choir, and a bass drum. The crowd really doesn't dig it. The player will wonder how these guys made it to Round 2 at all.
- **Round 3: vs Psychedelic Titanium, with Vance Thunderhorn.** Notably harder: the player needs 70% to 95% of notes, depending on prep (including optional, non-story prep). Players who lost Round 2 don't play it.
  - **Psychedelic Titanium** are a heavy-metal band. Jerks with huge egos and huge personalities, who treat the world like garbage. And completely forgettable: not terrible, but they'd never sell out a stadium. They only reach Round 3 because they brought in a famous guitarist and singer to co-lead on lead vocals and guitar, and the crowd knows him; many are fans. It's **Vance Thunderhorn.** He doesn't gel with the band at all (think of the funny song where Michael Bolton drops into a rap song to sing about Jack Sparrow and *Pirates of the Caribbean*), and they win on his popularity.
  - **Why Vance joined.** He didn't know they were jerks and found out too late. His arc: he's entering his sunset. His rock-star days are years behind him, but he doesn't want to let them go. At all. His band broke up years ago; rather, they dumped him when he got too far into Neon Drinks. He hasn't won a battle of the bands in twenty years. When the chance to compete came up, something he never thought he'd enjoy again, he wanted to relive the glory days, only to discover this indie metal band really are jerks, and not very talented.
  - **Their song: "We do what we want, so pay us big!"** When Vance sings the chorus, it doesn't match the rest of the song at all. He's just complaining that he still hasn't got his **Neon Red Scotch**: some double-crossing, two-bit hacks of a metal band had better get him his Neon Red Scotch, or there'll be some broken notes to pay! It's humorous, very well written and performed, and he's singing about them, but they don't realise it until the end of the second chorus.
- **The beach and the locked gate.** Whether the player lost Round 2, lost Round 3, or won both, this sequence triggers before the next round.
  - They head to the beach. A tall, 12-foot metal gate is wedged open. As they pass through, Rex kicks the wedge out: "It ain't locked, so who cares." (It is locked.)
  - A series of fights break out, because of the encroaching Hush. Someone notices the sky looks different off in one direction, as if it's missing colour. Weird. Sable knows what it is and remarks that this is bad... but **inevitable**. ("Inevitable" is officially her word; at some point Eddar says it back to her in a cute display of affection.) Eddar shivers when he sees it.
  - **A mini-boss.**
  - Then all seems clear. Roxy, defiant: "The show must go on!" (Monsters aren't stopping this event.) They head back and find the gate locked. A poster warns that the top of the gate and fence are electrified, because so many monsters have been coming up from the sea.
  - Rex starts banging on the gate. Fade to white: **"X hours later"**, where X = 2 if the player lost Round 2, and X = 1 if they won or lost Round 3.
  - The gate is opened by **Three-Clear Fixer.** He lost Round 3 to what he humbly admits was the better opponent: "They got some dude in a fancy getup covered in notes... that guy is the coolest dude I've ever seen." (He means Leagues.) The heroes and Three-Clear talk a bit and head back to the battle.
- **Round 4: the final.** It plays out very differently depending on whether the player won Round 3, and both versions are rewarding. Throughout the round, the two finalists are constantly lit up by scores of flashes from people's old-school cameras.
  - **If the player won Round 3: vs Leagues and the Sopranos.** Apart from Leagues, the Sopranos are all dressed as mafiosi, in honour of the TV show. Extremely hard, and against a bunch of jerks too: the player must hit 100% of notes, and even then loses without all the side prep available.
    - **Luca.** A world event that lowers the difficulty: Rex can find and talk to the Sopranos' enforcer, **Luca** (one of their two bass players), and they get into a heated argument. If Rex chooses to hold back, Luca sucker-punches him, and the player earns the Award **"Keeping Your Cool."** Then, during the final song, Rex leans against the stage, smiling for a camera (someone recognised him). Luca, near the edge of the stage, sees this and kicks Rex. Rex gets on stage, punches him hard (a loud bass noise) and throws him into the crowd. Rex isn't technically part of any band, so this isn't cheating for anyone.
    - **Vito's invitation.** Leagues isn't lead vocals. Lead vocalist **Vito** starts improvising a tenor, opera-like solo before the song starts, singing about how he grew up listening to Vance Thunderhorn, looks up to the guy, and knows Vance can out-party anyone. But Vance got a bad deal: he joined a bunch of losers who give him Neon Pink Scotch instead of Neon Red. Why doesn't Vance pick up his guitar, like old times, and join a real band? There are no rules against it, and lo and behold, Vance comes on stage and joins them. This is one of the reasons the round is so hard.
    - **Their song has real soul.** A catchy beat, flashy (it is the 80s), but soulful. Leagues wrote it, and Vance's improvised additions are genius. At the end, Leagues and Vance embrace, and both go to the mic to say they look up to and madly respect each other. The crowd is all cheers, whistles and flashing cameras. Roxy is in tears: "That was so beautiful..."
    - **Why it's so hard.** It gives Leagues and Vance great dialogue later, as they constantly reflect on this win. They still have the dialogue if they lose, but it doesn't hit as hard: they're glorying in the participation, rather than being the old-school guys (none of this neon-everything and crazy looks, which is ironic, since both look crazy) who wound up champions, who might have to officially retire to give the others a chance, and who talk about the band they'll form together.
  - **If the player lost Round 3: Psychedelic Titanium vs Leagues and the Sopranos.** Leagues and the Sopranos always win, whatever the player does to interfere. Humorous ways to interfere are to be designed, so the sequence stays interactive.
    - **Psychedelic Titanium (with Vance) go first.** The metal guys look angry. Their song is **"Some has-been thinks he can tell us what to do..."**: no one tells them what to do; they're the baddest, the loudest, the wildest. They're clearly singing about Vance. They try to cut Vance from the chorus, but he drowns them out: "What morons give a guy Neon Pink Scotch, when he demands Neon Red? Both are available at the bar! These two-bit losers couldn't fill a gymnasium, they have so little talent..." Second chorus: "I told them to leave all vocals to me, because they're forgettable; no one buys their posters, mine still sell out 20 years later! And these hacks who can't play or sing gave me Neon Pink Scotch, not Neon Red!" Psychedelic Titanium's lines are forgettable, like a high-school band's; Vance's are on point, artistic genius.
    - **Leagues and the Sopranos go second.** This time Vito raps about looking up to Vance, listening to Vance, and how Vance should join them. The song is **"Keeping Your Word"**: about keeping your word to yourself and living life to the fullest. All of them rip on Psychedelic Titanium, Vance especially, in the improvised moments. It has genius-level lyrics, and it's more up-tempo and aggressive than their song against the player.
    - **Deep foreshadowing.** What they sing about parallels Nocturne keeping his word to Rex in Act V, bringing Rex and Lyra together at the Last Dance, and how love is freedom. The word "Nocturne" even shows up in the song, and so does "the Last Dance". Each chapter has a title shown in text, so the player will easily catch the parallel later.
  - **The big difference.** If the player is competing in Round 4, it's a real, soulful contest. If the player is watching, it's humorous, especially since the player can interfere.
- **If the player wins:** a reward and an Award (plus another Award for winning with only the original band members), with story and dialogue unlocks. The world changes: posters of Roxy's band turn up much more often, and people know who she is. In the Pink Wyvern, a large trophy appears in the stage room, with a giant photo of Roxy and her band on stage (the player sees it being taken). The photo is always the one from the original playthrough, so if Vance is in the band in Encore, he isn't in the photo.
- **If the player loses:** dialogue unlocks as Vance, Leagues and others talk to Roxy and her group about their talent. It depends on how well they played. At 95%+ of notes, Leagues might say: "I don't know how we beat you... If it weren't for Vance, we wouldn't have." Below 50%: "You know... for being as drunk as you guys were on stage, you weren't half bad... I'll give you that." (They weren't drunk on stage.)
- If the player lost Round 2 or 3 and didn't play Round 4, only the Aftermath trigger follows.
- **Encore and chapter select.** The player can bring in other Rockstar heroes, which makes winning MUCH easier and opens up a new song to play, with parts within it. Yes, that means Leagues and Vance can be in Roxy's band when the story has them in the opposing band; by this point Encore is established as breaking these kinds of continuity issues. With them in the group the difficulty drops massively: even with only the story prep done, 90% of notes wins.

## ACT II — "Crescendo" (≈7 h)

**Goal:** gather the **Five Verses**, ancient relics of the Resonance that the Canticle says the Hero needs to face the Darkness.

- **Thunderhorn** has already joined, after the Act I battle of the bands (Jesse, 2026-11-06: he joins as described there, not at Amplitude). The party reaches **Amplitude**, a stadium-city built around a colossal ancient speaker-temple. He sees Eddar's power at once and becomes his loud, loving mentor. The party gets an airship, Thunderhorn's tour ship ***The Encore***. Thunderhorn announces he's writing his last ballad "about you lot," and the party's adventures start showing up in his napkin lyrics.
- **Rex's Anthem (comedy side-arc, shaped by player choices).** Rex decides the Hero of Legend needs a battle song. Through dialogue choices the player can encourage, sabotage, or "help" him write it, and the lyrics change depending on what you pick. The best (worst) result is **"REX REX REX (Valor Forever)"**: a key change every eight bars, a rhyme of "Rex" with "Rex," and a kazoo solo. At the **Amplitude Grand Joust** it plays as his entrance song. The crowd winces. The party winces. Thunderhorn has to sit down. Rex is over the moon, and **in battle he gets a real buff whenever it plays**, while everyone else gets a small morale penalty. It becomes a running joke, and Rex's anthem can be equipped as a battle theme for the rest of the game.
- **The Verses** take the party across Halcyon: the **Neon Reef**, an underwater coral arcology; the **Frostline Observatory**; and the **Wildwood Speedway**, an overgrown racetrack in a jungle.
- **Rex is a disaster of a Hero.** He's hungover for one Verse trial, chasing a diver-girl during another, and one dungeon only gets finished because Eddar does the puzzle while Rex poses for a newspaper sketch. People start whispering about "the Hero's little friend."
- **Roxy's whirlwind:** she falls for a pirate captain, **Jack Cutlass**, who almost gets them all killed, and then cries on Eddar's shoulder about it. That's when Eddar realizes he doesn't feel what he used to for her. He feels *fond*. The person he wants to tell is Sable.
- **First Hurdle begins — Find Love (the slow burn).** On the deck of the Encore, during a meteor storm, Sable tells him she's been watching over him for years. She doesn't say why. Their hands brush on the rail. Neither moves away. They hold each other's gaze for one long second, until Thunderhorn bursts on deck shouting about a lyric, and the moment's gone. The Ledger: *"Entry #2,612: Her hand. The rail. One second. I have written fourteen love letters for another man and could not manage four words for myself. Physician, heal thyself."*
- **The Hero always dies (Jesse, 2026-11-05).** In Act II the party discovers that in every past cycle, the Hero of Legend died after the Scion of Silence was defeated and destroyed. No one knows why.
- **Act II climax: Nocturne's first appearance.** He takes the fifth Verse in person at the Frostline Observatory. It's a beautiful, frightening boss fight under an aurora. The party loses. Nocturne spares them. He speaks to Rex, the Champion, and looks past Eddar as if he were furniture. (Nocturne does not know what Eddar is until much later. For their first several meetings, Eddar is nobody to him. How and when he learns is still to be decided, but it is before the Act IV crater. From the moment he learns, he calls Eddar "Coda".)

- **"You took the wrong one" (Jesse, 2026-11-06; Act II or III, to be placed).** Grubb performs, perhaps at the Pink Wyvern, which was always his dream, and Aubade hears it. By now he's been counselled a bit about singing from the heart. His slam poetry, to a catchy beat, is about losing someone you love, the unfairness of life sometimes, and having to persevere. The song is called **"You took the wrong one."** Slam poetry is done in rhyme (any rhyming structure, not always AABB), so it's naturally of interest to Aubade, who speaks in rhyming pairs. In her rhyming way she says those were some mean rhymes, and kisses him on the cheek, before brushing him off as he blushes and freezes awkwardly. This is her first real opening up to him.

## ACT III — "Bridge" (≈7 h)

**Goal:** take back the Verses and save Solara from the Hush.

- **Sable's secret comes out.** Eddar asks her where he was born. She tells him half: she's a Rubric sister, assigned to watch him since he was six, and ordered to "silence" him if he ever woke up. She tells him she lied in every report to keep him safe. He believes her, and it still breaks something. **She doesn't tell him what happened when he was six.** She can't make herself say it.
- **The Siege of Solara.** Solara falls to the Hush. In a stunning set piece, Eddar holds a collapsing airship dock in the air with his mind while civilians escape, and he nearly dies doing it. Thunderhorn finally hits the high note to bring Eddar back from the edge, and it's the best moment of his life. That night he writes the ballad's second verse. Roxy fights beside Eddar the whole way, and starts to see him.
- **Lyra joins the party.** On the escape, she reads a note in Eddar's handwriting and goes very quiet. She doesn't say anything yet.
- **Why the Hero dies (Jesse, 2026-11-05).** The second scroll explains it as a balance. Draft wording, to be refined: *"There is no music in silence. There is no music in note. The World to be Saved requires the Scion defeated, and in the percussion of his cataclysmic destruction, the Hero too will be gone. And then the music will continue."*
- **Act end:** the Rubric arrive with an armada of red-sailed ships to "reclaim the instrument." They mean Eddar. Sable turns her power on her own order.

## ACT IV — "Dissonance" (≈7 h)

**Goal:** stop Nocturne from finding the last Verse and silencing all of Halcyon.

- **The Rubric hunts Eddar.** For the first half of the act, the party is on the run from the Rubric as well as the Hush. Sable fights her own sisters. At the **Rubric Abbey**, a red-ink monastery on sea cliffs, they find the **Breeding Codex**: three thousand years of marriages arranged across Halcyon's bloodlines, every one recorded in red ink, all leading to one name: **EDDAR**. The Rubric's goal was a Coda, a singer powerful enough to sing the Canticle's last verse and make it come true however they wished.
- **Eddar's power grows, and so does his anger.** He discovers he can do almost anything. He can stop sound, stop time for a heartbeat, or lift a Rubric ship out of the sea. He can also make a man's throat go silent. Every act of anger makes him stronger, and his eyes are violet more often now. The party starts to look at him differently. Rex doesn't. Rex, oddly, is the only one who isn't scared of him: "You're still Adder. I've seen you cry at a puppet show."
- **Roxy and Rex:** with Lyra now in Rex's life, Roxy lets Rex go and finds she doesn't mind. The Wildfires' big Solara show was cancelled by the war, so she plays it anyway, in a refugee camp, for free. It's the best show of her life. Eddar is in the front row.
- **Rex's betrayal (Jesse, 2026-11-04).** It's hilariously mundane. Rex simply can't make headway with Lyra. **Nocturne finds Rex and proposes a deal:** give him the scrolls, both verses of the Canticle, and in return he'll make sure the Princess is Rex's. Rex doesn't agree immediately, but it doesn't take much convincing. He hands the scrolls over.
- **Act IV climax: the Hush.** Nocturne lures Eddar to a crater of black glass in the mountains, a place where the Dirge has played, low and unchanging, for thirteen years, and birds won't fly over it. Nocturne waits at its center, unmasked: a scarred, tired, handsome man of fifty.
- **Second Hurdle — The Truth.** This is the "you are Revan" moment. Nocturne tells him: "This was Cantrel. Four thousand people and a temple of the Rubric. You were six. A sister punished you for singing the wrong note, and you got angry. You did *this*." Eddar's purged memories flood back, and the player relives them in a short playable flashback as a six-year-old boy: the temple, the red ink, a young novice Sable shielding a smaller girl, the anger, and then the first Dirge, and glass. "I have conquered nine kingdoms, boy," Nocturne says, "and I have never done what you did before you could spell your name. They didn't make a hero. They made the end of the world, and then they made it forget." Nocturne offers Eddar an alliance: break the Canticle together. Eddar refuses. But he walks out of the crater **different.** The reveal also exposes that **Sable, his budding love interest, belongs to the order that created him.** Sable and Eddar stop speaking.
- **The secret.** (Jesse, 2026-11-06: "the party is five" is shelved for now; 4 works better.) Grubb, Eddar, Sable and Leagues all know about Rex's betrayal. None of the others do, and the four keep it that way: "The people believe in Rex as their champion. Take away that belief, and the Hush has already won." (Eddar privately believes the people don't need the champion. They have him.)

## ACT V — "Coda" (≈5 h)

**Goal:** end Nocturne. End the war. And, as Eddar now believes, end the Canticle himself.

- **The confrontation.** Leagues, Lyra and others confront Rex: "All these people will die, because of you. You sold the scrolls to the Scion of Silence."
- **The twist: the scrolls never mattered.** This has to happen before the Last Dance (Jesse, 2026-11-04): otherwise Lyra would never dance with Rex, let alone kiss him, because he betrayed literally the whole world. Not long after, Nocturne interrupts and hands the scrolls back. "The scrolls are meaningless. I did what I did to prove a point. Taken away from this madness that's plagued our whole world for countless generations... look what you did?" He names what they did while the world thought it was lost, the things free people do. In the stretch of Act V before this scene, the heroes save people from monsters, gather what Roxy and Thunderhorn need for their songs, conclude people's personal stories, and do lots of side quests. There is also a scripted innocent moment that builds tension between the members of each couple. Nocturne's list draws on these (exact wording to be set in verse). The old "Danced, kissed" line is cut, because the Last Dance hasn't happened yet (Jesse, 2026-11-04). He looks to Leagues: "How did you come to think that the scrolls had any meaning to me, or to anything? Is it written in the Verses that the scrolls have great meaning? They're scrolls. Not even originals." He points at Eddar: "That boy destroyed the originals years ago." It's a role reversal. For once Rex's blunder has no negative consequence, and it shows that people considered wise, Leagues the archetypal Wise Man, can be egregiously wrong.
- **The Last Dance (also called the Grand Ball) — First Hurdle complete (Jesse, 2026-11-04).** It's now in Act V. Everyone thinks they've lost: Nocturne, the Scion of Silence whom the Hero of Legend always defeats, is going to win. People are furious. Why on earth is Rex the champion? He could never have beaten Nocturne, even if he hadn't traded the scrolls. Thunderhorn insists on one night off anyway: a ball in the ruined Amplitude stadium for soldiers, refugees and the party. It's the game's epic set piece, with massive build-up, played like FFVIII's dance scene. The player moves around and talks to people throughout. The stage carries on whether or not the player watches.
  - **Roxy opens.** Her song is the intro cutscene: super high-octane and mostly humorous, playing out as the player mingles. After it, she keeps playing other songs until a silent trigger.
  - **Thunderhorn's "One Word."** At the first trigger, Roxy actually exits the stage and Thunderhorn walks on and says a few opening remarks. The player can still move around; it's all just seen and heard if they watch. Then he premieres his ballad. It's much more real: about everything these heroes have been through, everything every hero since the beginning of time has been through, and what all people go through. What's the meaning of it all? One word (love). Thunderhorn still doesn't know the word. Each time he goes to sing it, something visual fills the screen, such as two people kissing or other acts of love, so it's obvious to the viewer. He only figures it out after Eddar dies. It's a much longer cutscene, but it still finishes with the player interacting with people. It's also the song that plays over the credits. After it, he plays other songs until a second silent trigger.
  - Rex is getting nowhere with Lyra, who is casually dancing with other men and women: anyone but him.
  - At the second trigger, Rex sees a man who looks just like Nocturne, but in a James Bond tuxedo and without the black aura. The man has Lyra's full, flirty attention. Rex walks over, angry. When he gets close, the man says: "Ah, just the Champion I wanted to see." Lyra rolls her eyes and blows her bangs out of her face.
  - The man vanishes into smoke and reappears on the raised stage where Thunderhorn just was. The lights drop to romantic and Nocturne's aura returns (he keeps the tuxedo). He talks about keeping his word: a deal is a deal. And when The End of the night comes, it's best to have lived in the moment. Free. (Freedom is a big thing for him.)
  - He sings something like "Unchained Melody". Black tendrils force Lyra and Rex into a slow dance, and everyone else falls into slow dances too; most don't even notice the new singer. The heroes see Nocturne and are uneasy at first, then start dancing with each other.
  - Sable, who hasn't spoken to Eddar since the reveal, sits on a bench. A couple of men approach to ask her to dance, and an unseen force humorously shoves them aside. Eddar approaches: "My dear..." Her eyes go wide, and she eagerly accepts his hand.
  - Dance and romance play out with the lyrics, and visions pass of the bigger, cosmic picture: Nocturne's song of love and living in the now is the space between the notes that makes the music.
  - **The instrumental break.** Partway through, Nocturne's song goes into just music, as many songs do.
    - Camera cuts to Sable and Eddar. Sable: "I should have told you. I'm sorry." Eddar shakes his head and even... almost... smiles. "I killed almost everyone you ever knew. I am the one who is sorry." (Delivered with his usual prosaic flair.)
    - Camera cuts to Lyra and Rex. Lyra, soft but firm: "I heard about the scrolls... they don't mean anything. But you did sell them, thinking they did mean something... why?"
    - Rex: "I didn't think they meant nuthin'. If they were so special, what makes me the Hero of Legend so special?" **This is the first time he ever says his title correctly.**
    - Lyra squints. The logic is both profound and not quite right. "OK... but why did you do it? You knew the whole world was on the line. That's what you were born to save. Why'd you do it?"
    - Rex ponders a moment. "I did it for you." Rex accidentally says something romantic.
    - Lyra blushes deeply, unable to take her eyes off his. "That makes no sense... but it's... sweet..."
  - **Aubade and Grubb (Jesse, 2026-11-06).** Aubade grabs Nocturne and kisses him, then slaps him, with a tear in her eye. She gives him the jewel-inlaid ring she'd been wearing (basically her wedding ring from him) and walks away. She finds Grubb, and they dance. At the song's conclusion he goes in for a kiss. She pecks him on the lips but keeps her hand on his cheek to prevent more, with a big smile on her face: **the first time we've seen her smile.** It's a very different feeling from Rex and Lyra (a teenage romance) and from Sable and Eddar (two scarred people finding a desert flower in all those proverbial harsh sands).
  - At the end, **Eddar kisses Sable.** **Lyra kisses Rex.** Any other romances that have come to a head kiss too. If the player kissed Roxy or Lyra earlier, that history is in the room: a look from Lyra across the floor. Roxy watches Eddar and Sable, and finally understands what she missed. The Ledger: *"Entry #2,977: She said yes. I am suspending the Ledger pending review."*
  - **Daybreak (Jesse, 2026-11-06).** After the three-kiss sequence, the camera fades up. It's night outside, and the side of the banquet hall is a floor-to-ceiling, wall-to-wall window (the ceiling is about 20 feet high). The screen fades to white, then fades to daybreak out on the countryside that could be seen through the window. The morning dew and mist are still on the land, and we pick up with the heroes. It shows the player that the heroes enjoyed the whole beautiful night.
- **Optional side scene: the Sovereign Ring (Jesse, 2026-11-06; after the Last Dance).** Eddar and Rex come across Grubb, cornered by Nocturne. Rex and Eddar prepare to fight, but Nocturne turns on them, clearly emotional. Speaking in verse, he demands to know: is Grubb a good man? Will he treat Aubade right?
  - It's a touching back and forth. Nocturne shows he still loves Aubade, despite all his endless cosmic prattle about ending the Canticle. Rex tries his best to talk Grubb up and fails horribly, because he's a buffoon. Eddar drops a deeply foreshadowing statement or three about how the Champion, the Scion... and a genetic genocidal freak meet and discuss love: "Let the next time the three of us face off be so lucky."
  - Grubb isn't scared. He tries to speak up, but Nocturne keeps punching the wind out of him with magic (nothing harsh). Grubb eventually gets out: "Beat me, hurt me all you want, kill me; and it won't stop my feelings for her any more than if someone did all this to you." Up to now Grubb mostly seems unmanly, with no spine. This is him showing he has one.
  - A line or two later, Nocturne puts the ring Aubade gave back to him into Grubb's hand: "This is the Sovereign Ring of our people. She who wears it is queen. Underneath that veneer of calm is a tigress you won't be able to handle. One who doesn't think straight, and forgot this wasn't just some act-of-union ring. Get it back on her hand." (Nocturne's lines are to be set in verse.)
  - Not long after, Rex, cracking his knuckles, says something to the effect of: "Alright... Grubb's got the queen's ring. You said your piece. Now, it's time I returned the favour for that butt whoopin' you gave me..."
  - Nocturne: "You are so wrote... And on that note..." He disappears into the ether. (Pun intended.)
- **Third Hurdle — Seduced by the Dark.** Eddar stops holding back. He crushes a Hush army with a single word. He walks into the Rubric Abbey alone and forces Mother Superior Carmine and the whole order to their knees, then burns the Breeding Codex with violet fire while the sisters weep. He's magnificent and terrifying, and he's winning. **His friends become afraid of him.** Thunderhorn stops calling him "kid." Roxy can't meet his eyes. Lyra keeps the party between Eddar and the civilians. Only Sable stays close, and even she flinches once, when he raises his hand too fast. He sees her flinch. (Unknown to anyone, Sable is pregnant.)
- **The Silent Citadel:** Nocturne's fortress, a black cathedral at the source of the Dirge. There is no melody here at all, only the drone, and the party's voices come through muffled. It's the most stylish dungeon in the game. When the party fights, their battle songs push back against the Dirge, so the music literally fights the place.
- **The final battle (Jesse, 2026-11-06).** It replaces the old order (an easy Nocturne fight, the last-page reveal, Da Capo, then a harder fight without Eddar).
  - **The place.** A tall bluff, like the fjords of Norway, with the landscape visible in every direction. In the immediate area, the final throes of a beautiful summer. Beyond it, in all directions, the darkened Silence: Nocturne's triumphant Hush. No birds, no movement, only dirge. The player can move the camera around, and looking at a darkened area brings up the dirge.
  - **Round 1: Eddar vs Nocturne.** Eddar and Nocturne face each other at the nape of the bluff. The other heroes are away and below (about 20 feet away, 5 to 10 feet down). It isn't really a fight: Eddar is vastly more powerful. The player feels how far outside the system he is now. He eclipses the Scion of Silence. In the build-up cutscene and this round, the heavens swirl around his levitating body, and the Otherworld-inspired theme from the end of Act I plays again (Jesse, 2026-11-06): Eddar is verging on becoming someone like Nocturne.
  - **Eddar seems lost.** A cutscene: Eddar yells at the defeated, dying Nocturne about the millions of lives lost in his insane quest to free those very lives, and he monologues. The other heroes think he's lost to his own power. Sable, who fell in love with him, is tearing up. Aubade turns her away: "Don't watch. I've already seen this play out with one man" (about Nocturne lost to his power-mongering; said in verse, since Aubade, like Nocturne, speaks only in rhyming pairs).
  - **Something is wrong.** Broken cymbals and out-of-tune pianos can be heard. Ripples come and go through the very air. Birds and animals in the nearby forest flee in terror.
  - **The revelation.** Nocturne laughs crazily. The Hero of Legend must defeat the Scion of Silence: that is the Crescendo, and there can be no other. By breaking this, Eddar is undoing the fabric of the music, reality itself. As it worsens, even Nocturne quietens, watching with understanding that this is bad. Infinitely bad. It isn't what he thought would happen: he thought it would bring freedom, not the end of existence.
  - **The sacrifice.** Eddar sacrifices himself, reviving Nocturne at the expense of most of his power and life essence. Reality stops falling apart. Nocturne rises and, unsure what to do, tells the heroes that perhaps freedom can only be bought with a change to the music: the Scion wins. He has already granted them freedom (the fiasco with the scrolls), after all. And no, he's not giving up his life just to fulfil this damnable Canticle.
  - **The heroes answer.** Lines of defiance; sorrow as Eddar lies dying; rage as the Hush presses in; and appreciation that Eddar didn't become a Nocturne and didn't give in to his power.
  - **Three movements.** The heroes charge, and three fights unfold, each with 4 heroes against Nocturne. Each fight is named after one of the first three movements of a symphony. The player splits the heroes between the three parties. During these fights Rex runs to Eddar's side and shields him with his body.
  - **The Hero's blow.** After the three fights, Rex finishes Nocturne. On the blow that clearly does him in, a loud, low bell tolls and a shockwave goes through the air. Rex convulses, blood comes from his mouth, he grunts in pain and stumbles back, saying the legend is true: the Hero of Legend must go with the Scion; it takes notes and silence to make the music. The other heroes, Grubb and Lyra most of all, are frantic with grief. Before they can reach Rex, Nocturne breathes his last and a much louder bell tolls.
  - **Eddar's last save.** Around Rex and the other heroes, time stops. They can all move, feel and see, but the world has stopped. Eddar appears, as Obi-Wan Kenobi does for Luke Skywalker. He looks at Rex and says his line about Rex always looking out for him; now it's his turn to look out for Rex ("It's my turn... to save you... big brother..."). Time restarts, the bell toll slams into Eddar, and the screen goes white as Rex and the others cry out.
  - **The last battle, "Crescendo": Rex and Eddar vs Nocturne** (Jesse: named for the final part of the symphony, after the three movement fights), mirroring the first tutorial fight. The battlefield is exactly where they were, but all the Hush and Silence is gone from the land. The world carries on, and the other heroes watch from nearby. It's a hard fight with the roles reversed: Eddar absorbs Nocturne's Silence and dark damage, while Rex heals with his special moves. The lines are all over the place, and towards the end Nocturne is serenely content: "And thus, the prophecy is fulfilled. With its end."
  - After the fight, the sad end scene plays out (the goodbyes below).

### The goodbyes
Eddar is lying on the bluff, with the Hush gone and colour back in the world. Everyone gets their moment:
- **Thunderhorn:** "You were the headliner, kid. Always were."
- **Roxy,** crying and furious at herself: "I should've— you were *always there*, and I—" Eddar: "That's what I was for. It was a good job."
- **Lyra** holds up the note in his handwriting. "It was you. All of it. Every letter." Eddar: "He meant them. He just couldn't spell them."
- **Rex (Jesse, 2026-11-05)** has more to say now. He blathers, crying severely, things like: "I'm not a hero. Not a champion. I sacrificed the whole world for some princess... You may not look it, but you're the Hero of Legend... little brother... please don't die. Let me die, no one needs a buffoon in a pompadour... Everyone needs you..." Eddar weakly grabs his arm: "The prophecy is fulfilled, you ended the Scion. And the Canticle ended with me... The End... Nothing More... for the legend... I love you, big brother. Thank you for always looking out for me..." Rex hugs him roughly, crying, losing control: "Not you... not you... not you...", until Lyra gently pulls him away. "I love you, brother," he says, pulling away to leave Sable with Eddar's final breaths. (Then Sable's exchange, as people watch on.)
- Everyone says it: they should have believed in him, and they were afraid of him at the end.
- **Sable is last.** "Why? You put your power next to the gods'. You never cared about prophecies. Why do *this*?" Eddar: **"I did it for you..."** He puts his hand on her stomach. **"And you..."** She didn't know. She understands. **"I... love..."** That's his last word: love.

## EPILOGUE — "Fine" (pronounced *fee-nay*, the musical term for "the end")
- Halcyon is free of the Hush. The Rubric is broken, and the Canticle has gone silent for the first time in three thousand years.
- **Player choices play out in end scenes:** which towns you saved, which side stories you finished, whether Roxy's band made it (it depends on the choices), whether Thunderhorn retired or toured, how Rex's Anthem turned out (it depends on how you helped write it), which Rubric sisters you spared, and how Eddar's Harmony and Dissonance choices are remembered.
- Rex and Lyra marry. Rex learns to read, slowly, and the first thing he reads aloud is Eddar's last letter, the one Eddar wrote for *himself*.
- Roxy plays a stadium in Amplitude. Her encore is **"Always There,"** written for Eddar.
- **Thunderhorn finishes the ballad.** He was there when Eddar said his last word, and it's the word he'd been missing all game. The full **"One Word"** plays over the credits, and this time he sings it: *love*. If the player chose to keep Rex's Anthem around, it gets one last, gloriously terrible reprise in a post-credits stinger.
- **Sable's child is born.** A girl. Her eyes flicker violet, then settle to brown.
- Sable opens the Unfairness Ledger. The last entry, in Eddar's hand: *"Entry #3,114: I got to love her. Ledger closed."*
- **Final scene:** a scroll covered in red-ink prophecy. An invisible hand writes over it in **black ink**:
  > *"Freedom is known by all, as the prophesies are ended upon one word; love."*
  - **Whose hand (Jesse, 2026-11-06).** The black ink is Nocturne's: by then the game has established his flamboyant black handwriting. **This is deliberately never explained** (unless there is DLC). Players are left to wonder: how is Nocturne still around? Didn't the Canticle say that the Hero and the Scion both live on, or neither does? Since Eddar's sacrifice let Rex live, is that how Nocturne survives? But didn't Eddar end the Canticle? Is there another reason he continues? When did he write "love"? Were we watching the present at all? The whole experience is a cycle that ends where it starts. Never answer these in game text.
- **The game ends.**

## "LOVE": THE FIRST WORD AND THE LAST
- **Eddar's first line (Jesse, 2026-11-06; was Encore-only).** On every playthrough, in his two-second first shot (the mop boy, just in from the street, looking in at the bar), Eddar says one word, dispassionately, right at the camera: "Love." It makes no sense in the moment. By the end it makes perfect sense: love is both the first and the last word of the journey, and the end is where the beginning is.

---

## Why this lands
- **A protagonist you root for from minute one:** he's competent, wronged, funny and kind, and he gets stronger every act. Jesse's note on v1 was that a pathetic hero kills the story, and this fixes it.
- **Rex stays the best bully ever written,** and he's now *also* the reluctant Hero of Legend. His betrayal (selling the scrolls to Nocturne for Lyra, which turns out not to matter) and his redemption (the final blow) make him a full arc.
- **Twists:** the Hero isn't the hero, the letters, Sable as watcher, the breeding program, Eddar destroyed his hometown, the Bringer of End is Eddar, and the sacrifice restores the villain, and the scrolls Rex sold were meaningless copies.
- **Melancholy:** the Ledger, Roxy's too-late song, Lyra's letters, the unborn child, and "love" as his final word.
- **Romance:** Eddar and Sable at the center, Rex and Lyra, plus Roxy's whirlwinds.
- **Betrayal:** Rex sells the scrolls, Sable's secret, and the Rubric that made him and erased him.
- **Gameplay mirrors story:** the player gets addicted to Eddar's power, and then has to win without him.
